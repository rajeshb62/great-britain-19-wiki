Sources Map

This page maps the wiki’s source pages by what they cover. The collection is strongest for parliamentary and political narrative, naval history, industrial and social inquiry, Church history, prices and credit, engineering, and mid-Victorian imperial questions.

For the specific period from the end of the Crimean War to 1865, begin with Walpole, Vol. VI, McCarthy’s A History of Our Own Times, Clowes, Vol. VII, Reed, Mayhew, and Wakeman.

Core Political Narrative

Royal Commission — City of London

  • second-report-municipal-commission-city-of-london-1837 — The Second Report of the Municipal Corporations Commission (1837) on London and Southwark and the London Companies: limits, wards, governing bodies, courts, freemen and liverymen, property and revenue, the Companies, and the evidence.
  • royal-commission-city-of-london-1854 — The 1854 Royal Commission into the Corporation of the City of London: constitution, government, courts, police, freemen and liverymen, property and revenue, the thirty-two recommendations, and the Minutes of Evidence.

Harriet Martineau — History of England

  • martineau-1800-1815 — A compact narrative of Britain from 1800 to the end of the Napoleonic War, useful as the bridge from the war state to the postwar settlement.
  • martineau-history-of-england-vol1 — Introductory volume covering 1790–1809: war, administration, domestic policy, and the opening phase of the nineteenth-century story.
  • martineau-history-of-england-vol2 — 1815–1826: postwar distress, the Corn Laws, radical agitation, Peterloo, Queen Caroline, Canning, the 1825 panic, and Catholic emancipation politics.
  • martineau-history-of-england-vol3 — 1827–1836: Canning, Wellington, the Reform Bill crisis, abolition of slavery, the New Poor Law, municipal reform, and early Victorian party realignment.
  • martineau-history-of-england-vol4 — 1837 to the Crimean War: Victoria’s early reign, Peel, free trade, Ireland, Chartism, 1848, Palmerston, and the road to war.

Sir Spencer Walpole — History of England from the Conclusion of the Great War in 1815

  • walpole-history-of-england-vol4 — Reform-era and early Victorian political narrative, useful for the 1830s–1840s transition.
  • walpole-history-of-england-vol5 — Mid-century narrative overlapping 1850–1878 in the earlier edition; useful for the Crimean War, the Indian crisis, and the move into late Palmerstonian politics.
  • walpole-history-of-england-vol6 — The main general source for 1853–1880 in the expanded edition. This is the strongest existing single source page for the period from the Crimean War settlement through 1865, especially the end of the Crimean War, India, colonial self-government, and imperial questions.

William Nassau Molesworth — The History of England from the Year 1830

  • molesworth-history-1830-1874-vols1-2 — Vols. I & II (1873), covering 1830–1854. Vol. I runs from the Reform struggle through the death of William IV (1830–1837); Vol. II covers the reign of Victoria from her accession through the Crimean War preliminaries (1837–1854). Strong on parliamentary politics, the Corn Law struggle, Chartism, and the Great Exhibition era.
  • molesworth-history-1830-1874-vol3 — Vol. III (1875), covering 1854–1874. The Crimean War, the Indian Mutiny, the Palmerston ministry, the Reform Bill of 1867, and the first Gladstone ministry. Molesworth’s narrative is especially full for the transition from Palmerstonian stability to the Liberal reforming era.

Justin McCarthy — A History of Our Own Times

  • mccarthy-history-of-our-own-times-complete-burt-1880 — One-volume Burt edition covering Victoria’s accession through the general election of 1880. For this wiki, chapters I–XLVII cover 1837–1865; chapters XXX–XLVII are the key post-Crimean sequence.
  • mccarthy-history-of-our-own-times-vol2-belford-1884 — Belford, Clarke Vol. II, beginning with chapter XXX, “The Lorcha Arrow,” and running through chapter LXVII. Its opening half covers 1856–1865: China, transportation, the Indian Mutiny, the end of Company rule, the Orsini crisis, Derby and Disraeli, the Ionian Islands, the French treaty, the American Civil War, the Alabama, Palmerston’s last victory, and Palmerston’s death.

Sir Archibald Alison — History of Europe

  • alison-history-of-europe-vol1 — 1815–1823: the post-Napoleonic settlement, Holy Alliance, Peterloo, France under the Restoration, Spain, Russia, and Louis XVIII.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol2 — 1823–1831: Greek Revolution, Navarino, France under Charles X, English domestic politics through the Reform crisis, and Poland.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol3 — 1831–1842: Reform, Germany, July Monarchy France, Chartism, Corn Law agitation, the Eastern Question, and British India to the Afghan disaster.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol4 — 1841–1851: Peel, the Bank Charter Act, Corn Law repeal, famine, 1848 in Europe, and the first Sikh War.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol5 — 1831–1837: constitutional Germany, France under Louis Philippe, England after Reform, and the Eastern Question.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol6 — 1806–1842: French, English, and Indian chapters reaching into the Melbourne and Peel period.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol7 — 1841–1848: Peel, finance, railway mania, Corn Law repeal, Chartist suppression, and the road to 1848.
  • alison-history-of-europe-vol8 — 1841–1852: China, India, the Afghan recovery, Scinde, Sikh wars, 1848 Europe, and the Second Empire.
  • alison-history-of-french-revolution-vol2 — 1799–1807: background for the Napoleonic system against which the early nineteenth-century British state was formed.

Naval War, Sea Power, and Maritime Strategy

  • james-naval-history-vol1 — Introduction and the French Revolutionary War to 1796; useful background on naval architecture and the early war at sea.
  • james-naval-history-vol2 — 1797–1799: Spithead and Nore, St. Vincent, Camperdown, the Nile, Acre, and the Anglo-Russian expedition to Holland.
  • james-naval-history-vol3 — 1800–1805: Copenhagen, Amiens, invasion preparations, Villeneuve’s chase, Trafalgar, and Nelson’s death.
  • james-naval-history-vol4 — 1805–1809: the aftermath of Trafalgar, Copenhagen, the Dardanelles, the Chesapeake affair, Basque Roads, and Walcheren.
  • james-naval-history-vol5 — 1809–1813: naval operations from the late Napoleonic war through the War of 1812.
  • james-naval-history-vol6 — 1813–1827: the end of the Napoleonic wars, War of 1812 episodes, Algiers, Burma, and Navarino.
  • james-naval-occurrences-war-1812 — A contemporary British account of the naval War of 1812 with comparative force calculations and official documents.
  • clowes-royal-navy-vol5 — 1803–1815: the Royal Navy in the struggle with Napoleon, including Trafalgar, blockade, administration, and minor operations.
  • clowes-royal-navy-vol6 — 1812–1856: War of 1812, peacetime navy, Algiers, Navarino, St. Jean d’Acre, Crimean War, steam transition, and polar voyages.
  • clowes-royal-navy-vol7 — 1857–1900: the essential post-Crimean naval source, covering the Second China War, Indian Mutiny naval dimensions, ironclads, later expeditions, and late Victorian naval transition.
  • mahan-sea-power-history — A strategic and theoretical source on sea power from 1660 to 1783, useful for interpreting the foundations of British maritime supremacy.
  • reed-our-iron-clad-ships — Edward J. Reed’s technical survey of British ironclads. Essential for the 1860s naval-technology transition from wooden walls to armour, steam, turrets, rams, and monitor-style ships.

Press, Libel, and Radical Politics

  • hone-three-trials — William Hone’s self-published report of the three Guildhall trials (18–20 December 1817) for political parodies on the Catechism, Litany, and Athanasian Creed. Primary source for ex-officio informations, special-jury practice, Abbott and Ellenborough charges, uncounselled defence by historical parody, three Not Guilty verdicts, and the 29 December 1817 liberty-of-the-press subscription meeting. This scan also binds later Queen Caroline–era Hone squibs (Non Mi Ricordo!, Political House that Jack Built, Queen’s Matrimonial Ladder).
  • hone-pamphlets-and-parodies — 1830 Chidley collection of nine Hone political pamphlets (1820–22 Ludgate Hill originals) with Cruikshank cuts: Political House that Jack Built, Queen’s Matrimonial Ladder, Miraculous Host, Caroline Form of Prayer, Non Mi Ricordo!, Political Showman, Man in the Moon, Right Divine of Kings to Govern Wrong!, and Slap at Slop (Bridge-Street Gang). Primary illustrated radical satire of the Queen Caroline and reform years.
  • hone-every-day-book — Hone’s 1826 Every-Day Book Vol. I: day-by-day calendar of popular amusements, customs, saints’ days, and seasons (full year), dedicated to Charles Lamb; 170 engravings; six indexes. Non-political antiquarian sequel to the trial and pamphlet years.
  • carlile-republican-harrison-bound — Composite IA dump: Hone Political House that Jack Built; Cobbett 1817 cheap weekly run; Carlile The Republican No. 2, Vol. I (3 Sept. 1819). Primary Republican source for the 16 August 1819 Manchester killings — Carlile’s letters to the Prince Regent (30 Aug.) and Sidmouth (1 Sept.), with the Sidmouth–Derby thanks of 21 Aug. printed in full; Atkins and Fitch letters. First Republican Peterloo locus in this wiki.
  • carlile-republican-vol8 — The Republican Vol. VIII (11 July–26 Dec. 1823), Carlile from Dorchester Gaol. Dedication to Spaniards in Exile; weekly freethought (Vice Society, materialism, Methodism, Swedenborgians, Quakers); Dorset prison addresses; Mechanics’ Institutes as temples of science. Not a Peterloo reportage volume.

Economy, Prices, Credit, and Public Finance

  • imlac-insecurity-of-british-funds — A postwar public-credit tract using Hume and contemporary commentary to discuss debt, funding, paper money, and bankruptcy anxieties.
  • carey-credit-system — Henry C. Carey’s comparative analysis of banking, credit, and confidence in France, Great Britain, and the United States.
  • gilbart-laws-of-the-currency-country-notes-1844 — J. W. Gilbart’s 1854 statistical paper on English country bank notes after the Bank Charter Act of 1844: seasonal “laws of the currency” for England, Scotland, and Ireland; lapsed issues; comparison with 1845 Scottish/Irish rules; recommendations against amalgamation into a single issue.
  • tooke-history-of-prices-vol1-2 — 1793–1837: prices, currency, seasons, war, and Bank Restriction from the French wars through the end of William IV’s reign.
  • tooke-history-of-prices-vol3-4 — 1838–1847: Corn Law repeal, Bank Charter Act, railway mania, currency debates, and the crisis of 1847.
  • tooke-history-of-prices-vol5-6 — 1848–1856: free trade, corn prices, railway construction, Bank of England practice under Peel’s Act, French finance, and gold discoveries. This reaches the end of the Crimean War but does not carry the story to 1865.

Industrial Society, Labour, London, and Social Inquiry

  • engels-condition-of-the-working-class — Engels’s 1845 account of industrial England, especially Manchester, factory work, urban conditions, Irish immigration, mining, agriculture, Chartism, and class relations.
  • marx-engels-communist-manifesto — Marx and Engels, Manifesto of the Communist Party (1848 programme of the Communist League; this extract Moore/Engels English lineage with prefaces 1872–1893). Bourgeoisie and proletarians, Communists and the proletariat, critique of contemporary socialist literature, position toward opposition parties; later notes on the Paris Commune and international circulation. Pair with Engels’s Condition for English industrial context.
  • mayhew-morning-chronicle-survey — Mayhew’s 1850 Morning Chronicle letters on London labour, vagrancy, lodging houses, trades, slop work, toy-makers, and doll manufacture.
  • mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol1 — London street-folk: costermongers, street-sellers, food, flowers, literature, manufactured articles, women, and children.
  • mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol2 — Further street-folk: second-hand articles, live animals, street-buyers, Jewish street traders, finders, sewer and night-soil work, chimney-sweeps, and crossing-sweepers.
  • mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol3 — Street trades and labour: rat-killers, performers, musicians, garret-masters, coal-heavers, dock labour, cheap lodging houses, watermen, cab-drivers, vagrants, and ticket-of-leave men.
  • mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol4 — Those who will not work: prostitution, thieves, swindlers, beggars, vagrants, and charitable/reformatory agencies. The Mayhew volumes are the key social sources for London in the early 1860s.

Engineering, Science, and Technology

  • babbage-decline-of-science — Babbage’s 1830 polemic on the Royal Society, British science, scientific institutions, and reform.
  • smiles-engineers-vol1-vermyden-brindley — Early engineering: drainage, water supply, and canals through Vermuyden, Myddelton, Perry, and Brindley.
  • smiles-engineers-smeaton-rennie — Harbours, lighthouses, bridges, docks, dredging, and civil engineering through Smeaton and Rennie.
  • smiles-engineers-vol3-metcalfe-telford — Roads, bridges, canals, Highland works, the Menai and Conway bridges, and Telford’s civil engineering.
  • smiles-engineers-vol4-boulton-watt — Boulton, Watt, Soho, the condensing steam engine, Cornish engines, and gas-lighting.
  • smiles-locomotive-stephenson — George and Robert Stephenson, colliery railways, the Rocket, Stockton & Darlington, Liverpool & Manchester, tubular bridges, and railway expansion.

Church and Religion

  • overton-english-church-1800-1833 — Church of England history from 1800 to 1833, before the Oxford Movement.
  • overton-church-19th — Another Overton source page focused on the English Church in the early nineteenth century, including institutional condition and church reform.
  • wakeman-church-of-england — Broad Church of England history into the late nineteenth century. For the post-Crimean period it is useful for the High Church revival, Eucharistic controversy, ritual questions, and the widening of religious conflict through the 1850s–1860s.

Best Reading Paths

From the end of the Crimean War to 1865

Read walpole-history-of-england-vol6, mccarthy-history-of-our-own-times-vol2-belford-1884, and molesworth-history-1830-1874-vol3 together. Walpole supplies a broad historical structure; McCarthy gives a continuous Victorian political narrative from the Congress of Paris through Palmerston’s death. Molesworth Vol. III provides a detailed parliamentary and political narrative for the same period with strong coverage of the Palmerston and first Gladstone ministries. Add clowes-royal-navy-vol7 for naval and imperial dimensions, reed-our-iron-clad-ships for naval technology, mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol1 through mayhew-london-labour-london-poor-vol4 for London social life in the early 1860s, and wakeman-church-of-england for Church controversies.

For the Napoleonic and postwar transition

Use Martineau, James, Clowes Vol. V–VI, Tooke Vols. I–II, Alison Vols. I–II, Imlac, and Hone (three trials, 1817) for press, libel, and radical political culture.

For industrial and social transformation

Use Engels, Mayhew, Smiles, Babbage, Tooke, Carey, and Reed.

For empire and overseas crises

Use Alison Vols. III–VIII, Walpole Vols. V–VI, McCarthy, Clowes Vols. VI–VII, and James where naval detail is needed.
