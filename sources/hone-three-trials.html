Hone — The Three Trials of William Hone

William Hone (1780–1842) — The Three Trials of William Hone, for publishing three parodies; viz. The Late John Wilkes’s Catechism, The Political Litany, and The Sinecurist’s Creed; on three ex-officio informations, at Guildhall, London, during three successive days, December 18, 19, & 20, 1817; before three special juries, and Mr. Justice Abbott, on the first day, and Lord Chief Justice Ellenborough, on the last two days. — London: Printed by & for William Hone, 67, Old Bailey, 1818. Epigraph: “Thrice the brindled cat hath mew’d!” (Shakespeare). Sold with the proceedings of the public meeting; price four shillings in boards (title-page imprint). Third-trial section separately marked “Tenth Edition,” price one shilling.

Self-published report of Hone’s three successive Guildhall prosecutions for political parodies on forms of Church of England worship. The Crown proceeded by ex-officio information (Attorney-General Sir Samuel Shepherd). Hone defended himself without counsel. Each jury returned Not Guilty. The Address is dated 23 January 1818; the third-trial “To the Reader” is dated 22 January 1818. Hone states that he intends a later enlarged royal-octavo report with full shorthand of the third day’s defence, Attorney-General’s reply, and Chief Justice’s charge, fac-simile coloured prints shown at trial, Notes and Appendix, subscriber list, and portrait—this 1818 volume is the shorter contemporary report “with the proceedings of the public meeting.”

The extract is 10,136 lines / 90,886 words from a University of Minnesota Library bound volume (call 942.07 H75a; AA/Hathi page-OCR dump, 248 page images; OCLC 785874045; AA hash 5b0ddd2b3c9563f1d02f66a849f53843). After the three-trial matter the same physical volume continues with later Hone political pamphlets of the Queen Caroline period (Non Mi Ricordo!, matter related to The Political House that Jack Built, The Queen’s Matrimonial Ladder, &c.), treated below as bound-with material in this scan.

Content Summary

Front matter (extract pages 1–10)

  • Library prelims — University of Minnesota bookplate and empty leaves; OCR noise leaf.
  • Title page — full style as above; three special juries; Abbott day 1; Ellenborough days 2–3; Old Bailey imprint; four shillings in boards with public-meeting proceedings.
  • Address (William Hone, 23 Jan. 1818) — Hone says he had meant to exhort fellow-citizens against parodying Scripture or forms of worship established by law, and is glad the intimation had effect. Ministers are “laughed out of Court.” He jibes at the Home Secretary and at the President of the Board of Control as “my brother parodist.” He announces intention to commence bookselling “upon a more respectable footing,” and to prepare at leisure an enlarged permanent report (shorthand of third-day defence, AG reply, CJ charge; narrative from stopping the sale through the public subscription; subscriber list; Notes/Appendix; correct copies of parodies read in court; reduced coloured fac-similes of prints exhibited; portrait by subscription; royal octavo).

Subscription for Mr. Hone (extract p. 12; meeting 29 Dec. 1817)

Meeting of the Friends of the Liberty of the Press and Trial by Jury, City of London Tavern, Monday 29 December 1817, Mr. Waithman in the chair. Unanimous resolutions: liberty of the press inseparable from purity of trial by jury; Hone’s acquittals as demonstration of that right; scriptural parody long practised (Luther, church dignitaries, &c.), so exception taken by present Ministers was for political purposes against the press; hypocritical use of religion as a mask for political persecution; object of the prosecutions was not to protect religion but to crush an individual who had exposed ministerial delinquencies, stifle discussion, destroy liberty of the press, and uphold abuse; praise of Hone’s knowledge, talents, intrepidity, and perseverance. (Hone’s third-trial “To the Reader” states that a report of the speeches at this meeting, with a list of subscribers, is added to that account.)

The First Trial — The Late John Wilkes’s Catechism (18 Dec. 1817)

  • Caption — The King against William Hone, ex-officio information for publishing the late John Wilkes’s Catechism; Guildhall, Thursday 18 December 1817, London Sittings after Michaelmas Term; before Mr. Justice Abbott and a special jury.
  • Court opening — Crowds from early morning; Hone enters with parcels of books covering the table; special jury sworn (foreman later John Godwin Bowring of Leadenhall Street; panel includes Godwin Bowring, Gardiner, Hilton, Brook, Hunter, Thompson, Lewis, Edwards, Syme, Woollett, O’Brien, Noakes).
  • Pleadings (Mr. Shepherd) — Information for an impious, profane, and scandalous libel on the Catechism, intent to excite impiety and irreligion and bring the Catechism into contempt.
  • Attorney-General (Sir Samuel Shepherd) — Christianity “parcel of the Common Law” (Hale); Book of Common Prayer annexed to statute under Charles II; the parody is a “Cutechism… to be learned of every person before he be brought to be confirmed a Placeman or Pensioner by the Minister”; Creed and Commandments parodied as courtier’s faith and Minister’s Ten Commandments (loyalty to place, hostility to Cochrane and Friends of the People, &c.). Cheap circulation among the “lower classes” stressed; even if meant as a political squib, tendency to contempt of religion remains.
  • Parody substance (as read/quoted) — Political catechism of placemen and pensioners; articles of belief including Jacobite/legitimist jibes and faith in Finance and Secresy committees and the “Pitt System”; Commandments addressed to “I the Minister… who brought thee out of Want and Beggary, into the House of Commons.”
  • Defence (Hone) — Long uncounselled speech. Narrative of arrest on Lord Ellenborough’s warrant, lock-up in Shire-lane, ignorance of the charge, pressure to plead; complaint of injustice by Ellenborough and of public misrepresentation; historical parade of scriptural and liturgical parodies (Luther and others) as evidence of custom and of non-prosecution; argument that intention was political, not to ridicule religion; family distress; assertion he would deny to his dying moment any design against the religion of the country. Court applause noted in the report.
  • AG reply — Prior parodies (even by venerable names) if they profaned Scripture were libels; a parody becomes libel when it excites ludicrous ideas about the thing parodied and brings sacred offices into contempt; intention gathered from acts; secondary effect still grounds guilt if tendency proved; cheap form accelerates mischief.
  • Abbott’s charge — Offence is publication with design/intent to promote impiety; if tendency is obvious on the face, law presumes contemplation of it; discontinuance of sale does not erase publication; Christian religion part of the law of the land; political application does not purge profaneness; quotes the commandment against taking the Lord’s name in vain; reads/comments early part of the libel, declines to shock the jury with the Lord’s Prayer parody; states his opinion that the production is highly scandalous, irreligious, and libellous, while leaving the verdict to the jury’s conscience; hands jury the publication, an official copy of the information, and a Prayer-book.
  • Verdict — Jury withdraws; returns in less than a quarter of an hour; Bowring as foreman: NOT GUILTY. Loud acclamations in court; Abbott asks rejoicing to be reserved for a fitter place.

The Second Trial — The Political Litany (19 Dec. 1817)

  • Caption — Second trial; ex-officio information for publishing “The Political Litany”; Guildhall, Friday 19 December 1817; before Lord Ellenborough and a special jury.
  • Scene — After the first acquittal, public expectation that the AG would drop remaining informations; instead he perseveres. Avenues blocked; court cannot hold a twentieth of the crowd. Hone arrives with large bundles of books. Ellenborough (unexpected after Abbott the day before) takes the bench; sends for the Sheriffs.
  • Jury — Only six special jurymen answer; Crown prays a tales; six common jurymen added after one challenge (John Austen, shoemaker, Aldgate, challenged by the Crown). Special jurors named (Wilson, Lindsay, Thornton, Gillman, Mackie, Black); talesmen (Jones, Smith, Thorne, Donaldson, Hale, Green).
  • Pleadings — Two counts: (1) impious and profane libel on the Litany or General Supplication; (2) wicked and seditious libel of and concerning the Prince Regent and the Houses of Lords and Commons. Plea: Not Guilty.
  • AG opening — Stresses the solemnity of the Litany; quotes parody openings: “O Prince, ruler of the people, have mercy upon us, miserable subjects”; “O House of Lords, hereditary legislators…”; “O House of Commons, proceeding from corrupt borough-mongers…”; “Son of George, we beseech thee to hear us” for “Son of God…”. Auditory laughter noted in the report.
  • Full text insert — The Political Litany (extract ~pp. 66–67 ff.) — “diligently revised; to be said or sung, until the appointed Change come, throughout the Dominion of England and Wales, and the Town of Berwick-upon-Tweed… 1817. Price Two-pence.” Supplications against unnational debt, unmerited pensions and sinecures, extravagant civil list, starvation, warlike establishments in peace, corrupt elections, pensioned hirelings of the press, distress for taxes, crowded jails, overflowing poor-houses, Parliament chosen by one-tenth of tax-payers; petitions for constitutional rule, economy of the Royal Family, equal representation, rights of citizens regardless of creed, &c.
  • Defence and reply — Hone again piles historical parodies; AG answers that historical profanation is no justification, that Hone was selected as leader of recent parodies, that stopping sale (stated as 27 February) goes only to mitigation, that the Litany is among the most beautiful compositions in the ritual and stands in the statute-book, and that Christianity is part of the common law (Hale).
  • Ellenborough’s charge — Delivers “solemn opinion” under the Act that the piece is a most impious and profane libel; hopes the jury as Christians will agree. Report notes his lordship much oppressed with indisposition; charge almost inaudible beyond the bench.
  • Verdict — Jury retires quarter past six; returns at eight; foreman NOT GUILTY. Reiterated shouts; Sheriffs cannot keep order; acclamations through Guildhall and King-street. Ellenborough asks AG what case next; AG: the Sinecurists’ Creed. Hone nearly crushed by greetings on leaving.

The Third Trial — The Sinecurist’s Creed (20 Dec. 1817)

  • Separate title (extract p. 105) — The Third Trial of William Hone… for publishing a parody on the Athanasian Creed, entitled “The Sinecurist’s Creed.” Tenth Edition. Printed by & for William Hone, 67, Old Bailey; price one shilling.
  • To the Reader (22 Jan. 1818) — Delay from constant callers from all parts of the country; thanks for unacknowledged marks of attention; adds proceedings of the City of London Tavern meeting and subscriber list as suggested.
  • Jury and preliminaries — Hone exhausted from prior days but elects to proceed. Special jury and talesmen sworn (Morewood, Elwall, Edgar, Eckenstein, Barry, Brockbank, Clerk; talesmen Lewis, Coles, Pearce, Sansum, Newman). Ellenborough warns objections to Crown challenges must be taken now; Hone waves objection—“The Jury are all strangers to me.”
  • Pleadings — Information for irreligious and profane libel on the Creed of St. Athanasius, intent to scandalize and bring it into contempt.
  • AG — Established form of prayer defended by law; doctrinal differences do not license scoffing; tendency is the question; deliberation in writing/publishing bars plea of impulse; similar works by others are no excuse.
  • Proof and reading — Purchase proved (Swanson, Treasury Solicitor’s office) at Hone’s late Fleet-street shop, 17 February. Hone insists St. Athanasius’s Creed be read before the parody. Full Sinecurist’s Creed (Quicunque vult; “From Hone’s Weekly Commentary, No. II”; two-pence): sinecurist’s faith as holding a place of profit; Trinity of ministry burlesqued as Old Bags, Derry Down Triangle, and the Doctor—coeternal folly, mountebanks, quacks, and fools; mystification of Derry Down Triangle as Minister and Manager; majority-with-ministry as the thing “to be worshipped.”
  • Defence (Hone) — Multi-hour speech (report: precisely eight hours and five minutes on this day, after long efforts on prior days). Political ground of the prosecution; Canning as ministerial parodist (Pitt as Elijah, mantle dropping); Fox lampooned in old prints; Sidmouth and Ellenborough as persecutors who would “crush the insect”; defiance—“They could only immortalize him”; Scott editing Somers’s parody without prosecution; Southey’s path from Wat Tyler to pension; taxes on children’s bread funding secret-service pursuers; solemn denial of intent to ridicule the Athanasian Creed; Gibbon/Warburton/Waterland/Tillotson and clergy petitions to Bishop Porteus on the Creed; jury not bound to the judge’s opinion; Fox’s libel act; liberty of the press attacked through him; two prior acquittals; hopes to dine Sunday with his family. Declines re-calling stoppage witnesses; Ellenborough reads his notes of that evidence; Thomas Cleary proves Hone’s stated intention (near Charing-cross, 21 February) to stop the parodies after the Lords’ Report called them profane and seditious.
  • AG reply — Duty to persevere; stoppage vs. claim of right; danger of republication if right admitted; judge’s right to state opinion on law of libel; distinguishes Hone’s citations (Reeves, Toplady, Scott’s zealots) from the present paper.
  • Ellenborough’s charge — Defendant’s grievance narrative groundless; AG’s discretion to separate charges; scriptural libel not a privileged class (Woolston, Paine); Fox’s Act did not strip the judge of stating the law; intent concluded from the act; reads the parody (Old Bags / Derry Down Triangle / the Doctor as Father / Son / Holy Ghost associations); question is whether ludicrous images attach to the Creed itself.
  • Verdict and aftermath — NOT GUILTY; spontaneous burst in court and hall. Crowd estimated in the report at not less than 20,000 in the Hall and avenues during the jury’s absence. Hone slips out alone and unnoticed after a decoy group is cheered. Side episode: Mr. Thomas Wetherell (King’s College, Cambridge; lately from the West Indies) brought up for riotous conduct on the steps after asserting right of entry to an open court; scuffle with City marshals.
