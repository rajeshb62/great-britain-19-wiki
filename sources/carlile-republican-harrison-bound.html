Carlile / Hone / Cobbett — Harrison-bound Radical Miscellany

Composite bound volume (library miscellany / binder’s gathering) whose Internet Archive plain-text dump runs Hone’s Political House that Jack Built, a long run of Cobbett’s Weekly Political Register / Weekly Political Pamphlet (1817, chiefly Vol. 32), and Carlile’s The Republican No. 2, Vol. I (3 September 1819) with the immediate post–16 August 1819 letters on Manchester—then unrelated later tracts. The dump is the working extract for the 1819 Carlile Peterloo-week Republican and the co-bound Hone and Cobbett pieces; it is not a clean single-title edition of The Republican Vol. I.

IA / Google-style OCR plain text; 38,093 lines / 239,051 words. Binder and shelfmarks in the scan tradition associated with Harrison-type radical gatherings (treat provenance as the IA text dump + internal imprints below). After Carlile’s No. 2 matter the file continues into non-series tracts (e.g. Rev. James Ashley letter to George Maxwell, 1797 Spalding/Fleet drainage controversy; later society/subscription and moral-essay leaves)—out of scope for this source page except as trailing bound-with noise.

Content Summary

1. Hone — The Political House that Jack Built (opening of extract)

  • Title block — The Political House that Jack Built. “A straw—thrown up to show which way the wind blows.” With Thirteen Cuts. Motto block from Cowper’s Task. London: Printed by and for William Hone, Ludgate Hill. Price one shilling. (OCR title year 1810 in this dump—treat as digitization/print error relative to the well-known 1819 Ludgate Hill political-house campaign; cuts and Doctor Slop dedication match the post-Peterloo / Queen Caroline-era squib.)
  • Dedication — To Doctor Slop, “in acknowledgment of many public testimonials of his filial gratitude,” and to “the Nursery of Children six feet high, his readers”; signed as the Doctor’s political godfather, the author. Note: the name “Doctor Slop” was conferred in Hone’s Buonaparte-phobia, or Cursing made easy… (reprinted, one shilling).
  • Form — Nursery-rhyme parody stanzas with Cruikshank (or associated) cuts: “This is the House that Jack built,” placemen, privilege, and public-office ladder in the usual Hone manner.
  • Relation — Same title as item 1 in the 1830 Chidley collection hone-pamphlets-and-parodies; this opening is an earlier separate printing bound at the head of the miscellany, not the full nine-pamphlet Chidley volume.

2. Life of William Cobbett (biographical insert)

  • Caption Life of William Cobbett. Autobiographical narrative first framed as written in America, 1797: no illustrious ancestry; grandfather a day-labourer (scythe, reap-hook, flail); father a farmer who drove plough for twopence a day and learned land-surveying; Cobbett born 9 March 1766; three brothers; earliest work scaring birds from turnips and rooks from pease. Contrast with Franklin’s etymological family pretensions. Serves as preface/companion matter before the Register run in this binding.

4. Carlile — The Republican, No. 2, Vol. I (3 September 1819) — Peterloo letters

Masthead (extract ~L27442): The Republican. No. 2. Vol. 1.] London, Friday, Sept. 3, 1819. [Price 2d. Printer line: R. Carlile, 55, Fleet Street, London.

This is the first Republican publication in the wiki set that carries Carlile’s direct response to the 16 August 1819 Manchester meeting and killings (the “Peterloo” events—contemporary text says Manchester, yeomanry, magistrates; does not use the later nickname as title).

A. Letter to H.R.H. the Prince Regent

On his thanking the Magisterial and Yeomanry Assassins of Manchester for Murders committed by them on the 16th of August last.
London, 30 August 1819. Signed Richard Carlile.

  • Opens on public indignation at murders by Yeomanry Cavalry at the instigation of Manchester magistrates upon inhabitants in a legal and peaceable meeting for redress of grievances and radical reform of representation—exceeded only by the Chief Magistrate’s thanks to the “murderers.”
  • Inserts full Sidmouth / Whitehall letter, 21 August 1819, to the Earl of Derby: Prince Regent’s “great satisfaction” at magistrates’ “prompt, decisive, and efficient measures” for public tranquillity; “high approbation” of Major Trafford and the officers and men under him.
  • Argues that only the offenders’ own account was heard; hundreds of disinterested spectators not sought; violation of English jurisprudence and “moral decency.”
  • “Prompt, decisive, and efficient measures” glossed as cutting down a peaceable defenceless people; future historian to rank the Regent with the most ferocious Deys of Algiers; black border on the page that records an English prince’s sanction of the deed.
  • Blood of murdered countrymen cries for vengeance; people exposed themselves under the “fatally supposed mildness” of the regime; wipe the stain or face an insulted people; frenzy of a starving people.
  • Alternative: restore Justice and sweep corrupt influence, or the people recal delegated power and acknowledge no sovereign but their own authority; denial risks the fate of Charles or James.
  • Timing proof that the Regent could not have deliberated: news could not reach the Home Office before evening of the 17th; Regent then ≥100 miles from the metropolis (or at sea); yet Sidmouth thanks the “murderers” in the Regent’s name on the 21st—proof that Sidmouth anticipates and stamps the Regent’s will.
  • Expensive monarchy, standing army, fundholder vs. artisan and agriculturist, open-day murders by holders of the sword of Justice receiving sanction of constituted authorities—stage at which liberal writers have argued the justice of a people’s dernier resort force and beginning de novo.
  • High treason charge against meeting leaders softened to conspiracy and misdemeanor—pitiful before a jury; traitors in the Cabinet.

B. Second Letter to Lord Sidmouth

On the conduct of the Magisterial and Yeomanry Assassins of Manchester, on the 16th August, 1819; and on his Lordship’s conduct as subsequently connected with that Assassination.
London, 1 September 1819.

  • Refers to Carlile’s public letter of 18 August giving a narrative as spectator of the “horrid massacre”; thanks Sidmouth for the notice that produced police action—Morris the police officer swearing the letter an unlawful, inflammatory, seditious libel before Lord Mayor Atkins; Attorney-General prudence and bail after four days’ “conclave.”
  • Sidmouth (Home Secretary; Society for the Suppression of Vice; High Steward associations listed in invective) accused of preparing yeomanry minds, then approving murder without enquiry; hope of future impeachment when the state of things changes.
  • Metropolitan police farce: placard-holders seized while authors speak freely (Crown and Anchor meeting on the Manchester massacre; Kennington Common “Order” board / postponed meeting / Smithfield appeal to the Regent—man imprisoned, bill found, jury acquits).
  • Manchester magistracy and police: want of feeling; blood spilt at Manchester not washed away by libel-hunting in London.

C. Letter to John Atkins, Esq., Lord Mayor of the City of London

(continues the No. 2 sequence; satire on mayoral warrant process and choice of bail if Sidmouth orders another warrant). Closes toward R. CARLILE.

D. Supporting letter in the same number

Joseph Fitch, Old Road Academy, Stepney, 31 August 1819 — “To Mr. Carlile”: thanks for firmness against exactions; contest between government and people, Vice Society and liberal public, boroughmongering despotism and universal liberty; confidence London juries will not rear an Inquisition on the ruins of Magna Charta; Reformation memory and the press; “prove all things.” Printer line again Carlile, 55 Fleet Street.

5. Trailing bound-withs (out of core scope)

After the Republican leaves (~after L28253 printer line): Letter to Mr. George Maxwell from the Rev. James Ashley, Rector of Fleet (second edition; printed by John White; gratis from printer / rectory / King’s Head Spalding; internal date frame 1797)—South Holland Drainage and Fleet Inclosure commissioner disputes. Further leaves include subscription/donation tables, American imprint notes, and moral/religious essay extracts (war, peace societies, Erasmus, &c.). Not treated as part of the Carlile–Hone–Cobbett radical core for indexing.

Scan completeness audit

  • Hone Political House: title, dedication, and opening stanzas present at head; not audited here as a complete standalone pamphlet transcription (prefer hone-pamphlets-and-parodies for the nine-title set).
  • Cobbett 1817: long continuous cheap-weekly prose; mastheads and February–September 1817 dates recoverable; OCR long-s and broken words heavy.
  • Republican No. 2: title, three Carlile political letters, Sidmouth Whitehall insert, and Fitch letter recoverable as continuous argument; page folios “THE REPUBLICAN” / “SEPTEMBER 3, 1819” visible in OCR.
  • Tail: Ashley–Maxwell 1797 and later leaves are foreign to 1817–19 radical core—ignore for entity work unless a separate source is opened.
  • Quality: adequate for quotation of Carlile’s Regent/Sidmouth argument and for identifying Hone/Cobbett co-binds; not clean library typeset.
