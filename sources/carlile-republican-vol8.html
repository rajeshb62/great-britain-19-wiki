Carlile — The Republican, Volume VIII

Richard Carlile (1790–1843) — The Republican, Volume VIII, from 11 July to 26 December 1823. London: Printed and Published by R. Carlile, 84, Fleet Street, 1823. Weekly numbers (price 6d.); imprint footers also give 5, Water Lane, Fleet Street, with correspondence directed to 84 Fleet Street or Dorchester Gaol. Epigraph (title-page): restraint of writing and speaking makes men “less than men, less than any kind of animals; for all other animals have the liberty of speech.”

Bound half-year of Carlile’s weekly freethought and republican journal, written principally from Dorchester Gaol in the fourth–fifth year of his imprisonment for publishing Paine’s theological works and related deist/atheist tracts. The volume is dedicated “To Spaniards in Exile” after the collapse of the Spanish constitutional regime (1823 French intervention; death of Riego). Carlile urges Spanish patriots to examine Christian foundations, break priestly influence, and aim at an avowed republic rather than constitutional monarchy. He presents the volume’s leading feature as morality uncontaminated with religion, and recommends all eight volumes of The Republican to the exiles once they can read English.

The extract is 45,123 lines / 366,587 words from a Harvard College Library Google Books digitization (Wendell Fund; IA identifier republican08carl; Google/IA OCR plain text). Continuous weekly issue structure is recoverable; long-s / broken-word OCR noise is frequent; Google watermark and digitization prelims precede the title.

Front matter

  • Google / Harvard prelims — public-domain usage guidelines; Harvard College Library bookplate; Evert Jansen Wendell Fund.
  • Volume title — The Republican, Volume VIII, 11 July–26 December 1823; Carlile, 84 Fleet Street, 1823; liberty-of-speech epigraph.
  • Dedication — To Spaniards in Exile — Defeat blamed above all on absence of free discussion and on religious orders as enemies of the constitution; priests fomented insurrection and called in French aid with Ferdinand; church-property applied to national wants made reformers “heretics”; next Spanish revolution must end in republic; monarchy and freedom incompatible; constitutional monarchy contains the germ of civil war. Theological part of the volume denies supernatural designing power beyond the animal world and equates religion with idolatry (“not so much atheism as idolism”). Riego’s fate used against truce with Ferdinand. Attention directed especially to No. 1 (Cartwright / constitution). Signed Richard Carlile.
  • Contents of Volume VIII — issue-by-issue list opening with No. 1 review of Major Cartwright’s English Constitution produced and illustrated.

Issue map (Nos. 1–25, July–December 1823)

Digests follow the weekly numbers as recovered from headers and openings in the extract (not a publisher’s chapter scheme). Intermediate numbers without a distinctive lead letter are noted briefly.

No. 1 — Friday 11 July 1823

  • To the Republicans of the Island of Albion — review of Major Cartwright’s English Constitution produced and illustrated against Paine’s challenge to Burke (“no such thing as a Constitution exists in England”).
  • Fielding and Acts mottoes; constitution treated as fact, not name.

No. 2 — early August region

  • Subscriptions / acknowledgements block; ongoing prison correspondence.

Nos. 3–4 — No. 4 Friday 1 August 1823

  • To the Vice Society — defiance of the Society for the Suppression of Vice begging for prosecution money.
  • Near four years a prisoner for Paine’s Theological Works and Palmer’s Principles of Nature, both still sold in London; sister twenty-one months a prisoner.
  • Challenge that he can raise defendants as fast as they raise funds.

Nos. 5–10 — August to mid-September

  • Continued gaol letters, theological and political essays, subscription notices (imprints Water Lane / Fleet Street).

No. 11 — Friday 19 September 1823

  • Materialism versus Methodism — to Joseph Butterworth, Fleet Street bookseller, Methodist, and MP.
  • Methodism has neither moralized nor mollified him; clash with Hume on toleration of sects.

No. 12 — Friday 26 September 1823

  • To the Rev. William Wait, A.B., King’s Square, Bristol — insolent letters since imprisonment.
  • Still “Christianity’s Prisoner” after nearly four years; Wait’s 1820 insinuation that publications bring men to the gallows; Christians cast as the criminals who rob and cut throats.

No. 13 — early October

  • Intermediate number (gaol / theological series).

No. 14 — Friday 10 October 1823

  • To the Inhabitants of the County of Dorset — first address.
  • Mock loyal replies (King’s Arms waiter, Vicar of Cerne, magistrates, gaoler, postmaster Pitt); locked up as blasphemer and seditionist so that none may be “corrupted” by seeing him.

No. 15 — mid-October

  • Continuation of Dorset / prison series.

No. 16 — Friday 24 October 1823

  • To Mrs. Elizabeth Fry, St. Mildred’s Court — addresses Quakers as Society of Friends.
  • Free of the reproach he levels at persecuting sects; moral instruction characteristic; questions the foundation of the society while praising non-persecution.

Nos. 17–18 — late October to 7 November

  • No. 18 dated Friday 7 November 1823 (header); prison and freethought continuations.

No. 19 — Friday 14 November 1823

  • To the Inhabitants of Dorset again — nature of the conduct that made him prisoner four years.
  • No kindness from gaol management; simple natural opinions vs. spiritual catalogue (God, Christ, Holy Ghost, angels, devil, heaven, hell).

No. 20 — Friday 21 November 1823

  • To the Rev. Robert Hindmarsh, Swedenborgian priest, New Jerusalem Temple, Salford, Manchester.
  • Materiality infinite and eternal vs. Spirituality; Swedenborg and Clowes On the Two Worlds; German metaphysics and “diseased imaginations.”

No. 21 — late November

  • Intermediate number.

No. 22 — Friday 5 December 1823 (header OCR “1833”)

  • To the Inhabitants of Dorset — exposures driving county “high bloods” mad; bark then bite.
  • Prepared for destruction since Republican of November 1819; unpublished documents on gaol treatment; James Dare death / inquest correspondence with Visiting Magistrate Wollaston (cross-ref. No. 12 documents).

Nos. 23–24 — mid–late December

  • Closing prison and political numbers.

No. 25 — Friday 26 December 1823

  • Philosophical Institutions for the Improvement of Mechanics — Mechanics’ Institutes in London, Edinburgh, Glasgow (Glasgow: 1,000+ subscribers, 700 volumes purchased, 400 donated).
  • Substitute for public worship: halls or temples of science; as institutes increase, places of worship deserted as idolatry; Leeds Mercury lecturer obstacle dismissed—experiments and printed lectures preferred.

Recurring themes across the half-year

  • Prison testimony — Dorchester conditions; visiting magistrates; local clerical and loyal hostility; sister’s imprisonment; open sale of prosecuted titles in London despite confinement.
  • Vice Society war — prosecutions for irreligious publication as contest of stamina and subscriptions, not of argument.
  • Constitution and republican theory — Cartwright answered with Paine; Spanish lesson against monarchical compromise; laws and elective magistrates as sufficient government.
  • Sectarian addresses — Methodism (Butterworth), Church clergy (Wait), Quakers (Fry), Swedenborgians (Hindmarsh)—each used as a named medium to the public.
  • Materialism — denial of spiritual entities; morality without religion; idolatry as the genus of worship.
  • Popular instruction — Mechanics’ Institutes as the constructive alternative to chapel and church.
