A History of Prices and of the State of the Circulation, Vols. V–VI — Tooke and Newmarch (1857)

Authors: Thomas Tooke, Esq., F.R.S., and William Newmarch, Esq. Title: A History of Prices and of the State of the Circulation, during the Nine Years 1848–1856; forming the Fifth and Sixth Volumes of the History of Prices from 1792 to the Present Time Edition: Longman, Brown, Green, Longmans, and Roberts (London, 1857); with Adelphi Company reprint (New York, 1857) covering Parts VI–VII, the Appendices, and the General Index Coverage: 1848–1856 Raw text: raw/tooke-history-of-prices-vol5-6.txt (124,461 lines)

The final volumes of the History of Prices, now entirely under William Newmarch’s authorship though still published under Tooke’s name. Volume V examines the domestic British economy during the nine years after the repeal of the Corn Laws — corn prices under free trade, general produce prices, the railway mania and its aftermath, the progress of free trade since the Merchants’ Petition of 1820, and the operation of Sir Robert Peel’s Bank Charter Act of 1844 through the commercial crises of 1847 and beyond. Volume VI (Parts VI–VII) shifts to international dimensions: the financial policy of France since 1848, the Bank of France and French credit institutions, and the great influx of precious metals from California and Australia (1848–1856). A comprehensive set of appendices and a General Index to all six volumes conclude the work.
