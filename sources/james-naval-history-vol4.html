William James — Naval History of Great Britain, Vol. IV (1793–1820)

Author: William James (d. 1827) Title: The Naval History of Great Britain, from the Declaration of War by France in 1793, to the Accession of George IV Edition: A New Edition, with Additions and Notes (London: Richard Bentley, 1859) OCLC: 235925473 Volume: Vol. IV (of 6) Period covered: 1805–1809 Raw text: wiki/raw/james-naval-history-vol4.txt (24,342 lines)

Description

Volume IV of William James’s Naval History of Great Britain opens in the immediate aftermath of Trafalgar (October 1805) and covers the years through 1809. It follows Sir Richard Strachan’s pursuit and capture of Dumanoir’s escaped Franco-Spanish squadron, then proceeds year by year through fleet dispositions, frigate and single-ship actions, boat expeditions, and colonial campaigns across the Atlantic, Mediterranean, Baltic, and East Indies. The volume includes the Danish expedition to Copenhagen (1807), the Dardanelles operation (1807), the Basque Roads attack (1809), and the Walcheren / Scheldt expedition (1809), concluding with an appendix of annual abstracts of the Royal Navy’s ships.

Key Events

  • 1805: Strachan’s capture of Dumanoir’s squadron (completing Trafalgar); frigate actions Cleopatra vs Ville-de-Milan, Phoenix vs Didon

  • 1808: Spanish uprising against Napoleon; Convention of Cintra; Anglo-Swedish operations in the Baltic; war with Russia; Amethyst’s capture of Thetis; Seahorse’s action with Badere-Zaffer
  • 1809: Basque Roads (Aix Roads) attack; Walcheren expedition; capture of Anholt; Collingwood’s last year in the Mediterranean
